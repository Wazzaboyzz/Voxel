# CHAPTER TWENTY-TWO

Odette was lying to her. Mara was almost certain of it by the third day after the confrontation at Kell's, and the certainty sat in her stomach like a stone she couldn't put down, heavier for how much she didn't want to be carrying it at all.

It started small. Odette had always kept the store's back office locked during business hours, an ordinary precaution for a woman who handled the till, but that week she'd started locking it the moment she arrived in the morning, before a single customer had come through the door, and she'd started flinching, visibly, whenever the bell over the front door rang unannounced. Mara noticed because noticing was what she did, the same instinct that caught a hairline crack in a load-bearing wall before anyone else in the room had registered that anything was wrong. She'd spent enough of her career learning to trust that instinct that she no longer bothered arguing herself out of it, even when, as now, some quieter part of her wished she could.

There were other small things too, once she started looking. Odette had stopped eating lunch at the counter where anyone could see her, retreating instead to the back office with the door pulled to. She'd started keeping her phone face-down on every surface, a habit Mara had never once seen from her before this week. And twice now, walking past the feed store on unrelated errands, Mara had caught sight of her through the window of the old payphone booth out front, shoulders hunched, voice too low to carry, ending the call abruptly the moment she noticed she'd been seen.

"You've been avoiding me," Mara said, catching her alone restocking canned goods on a slow Tuesday afternoon, the store empty enough that their voices carried in the quiet between the shelves.

"I've been busy." Odette didn't look up from the shelf, her hands moving with more precision than the task required, each can placed with a deliberateness that felt less like tidiness and more like a woman giving her hands something to do so her face didn't have to answer for itself.

"Odette."

"Mara, I don't have time for this today." The words came out sharper than Odette usually allowed herself, and something in the sharpness told Mara she'd struck closer to a nerve than she'd intended, the kind of overcorrection that came from a person working hard to keep a much larger reaction contained.

That evening, Priya confirmed what Mara had already begun to suspect on her own, arriving at the orchard house with a look on her face that told Mara this wasn't a social call before she'd said a single word. "Odette's been making calls," Priya said, settling at the kitchen table across from Mara and Caleb, her voice pitched low even though there was no one else in the house to overhear. "Long ones. To a number outside the valley I can't trace easily, which on its own doesn't mean anything, except she's been doing it from a payphone outside the feed store instead of her own cell, which absolutely means something."

"A payphone," Caleb repeated, something hard settling into his voice. "Who still uses a payphone unless they're trying not to be tracked."

"That's what I thought too." Priya's jaw was tight. "I'm not saying she's working with Kell. I genuinely don't think that's it. But she's hiding something, and she's hiding it carefully enough that it's starting to look a lot like the kind of careful Kell himself is capable of."

Mara felt the accusation settle over the room like a weight she didn't want to hold, the particular sickness of suspecting someone who had, up to this point, been one of the only steady, trustworthy presences in a town full of careful lies. She thought of Odette's hand on hers over the store counter weeks ago, the unexpected tenderness of it, and hated how easily her mind now reached for the possibility that the tenderness had been a performance all along.

"I want to be wrong about this," Mara said quietly.

"So do I," Priya said. "But we don't get to only chase the suspicions that are comfortable."

---

They confronted her three days later, at the store, after closing, the three of them, Mara, Caleb, and Priya, standing in the small back office where Odette had been locking herself away all week, surrounded by the store's ordinary clutter of invoices and inventory sheets, the fluorescent light overhead humming faintly in the silence that followed the question.

Odette didn't deny the calls. She didn't do much of anything at first except sit very still behind her desk, her hands folded in front of her with a stillness that reminded Mara, uncomfortably, of Kell's own composure the night at his kitchen table, the specific stillness of someone deciding how much of the truth a room could survive hearing.

"You think I'm working with him," Odette said finally, and it wasn't a question.

"I think you're hiding something, and I don't know what else to call it," Mara said, hating how the words tasted even as she said them. "You've been careful in exactly the way he's careful. Payphones. Locked doors. Flinching at the bell. If it's nothing, Odette, tell us what it is, because right now the silence is doing all the talking for you, and it isn't saying anything good."

Odette closed her eyes for a moment, and when she opened them, something in her had shifted, the guardedness giving way to something rawer and more tired underneath it. "It's not Kell," she said. "God, Mara, it was never Kell. I would sooner burn this store to the ground than help that man with anything."

"Then what."

"It's Thomas." Odette's voice cracked slightly on the name, and she pressed her hands flat against the desk, the same bracing gesture Mara had watched Caleb make a dozen times this summer, the family resemblance in it striking despite the two of them not sharing a drop of blood. "Your grandfather. He asked me, months before you came back, before any of this started, to make certain calls if his condition worsened past a certain point. Calls to an old colleague of his, someone from outside the valley who helped him with the original 1986 investigation, someone he trusted to know what to do with his research if he didn't live long enough to finish it himself."

Mara felt the ground shift under the accusation entirely, the shape of the last three days rearranging itself into something she hadn't expected at all. "Why the payphone. Why all the secrecy, if it's just his old research."

"Because he made me promise not to tell you until he was ready, and because the man I've been calling made it very clear he didn't want his involvement traced back to him digitally, not after what happened to Elias Frey, not after watching what this valley does to people who dig too close to the truth without protection." Odette's eyes were wet now, and she didn't bother wiping them. "Thomas is dying, Mara. He knows it. He's been trying to make sure someone outside this valley has his research as insurance, in case whatever's coming for you and Caleb comes for you both before you finish this, and he asked me to be careful enough that even I couldn't accidentally lead anyone to that insurance policy before it was needed."

The office went very quiet, the fluorescent hum overhead suddenly the loudest thing in the room, and Mara felt something in her chest crack open, grief and relief tangled together so tightly she couldn't tell where one ended and the other began.

"You should have told me," Mara said, her voice unsteady now too.

"I know." Odette reached across the desk, and Mara let her take her hand this time, no hesitation in it at all. "I'm sorry. I was so afraid of getting it wrong, of either breaking his trust or putting you in more danger than you were already in, that I ended up looking exactly like the thing I was trying to protect you from. That's on me, not you. You were right to be suspicious. I just wish being right hadn't cost you three days of thinking I might be one more person in this town who'd lied to you."

Caleb, who had been quiet through most of it, finally spoke, something gentler in his voice than the hard edge it had carried walking in. "The colleague. Does he have the research now?"

"Most of it. Enough, Thomas says, that if something happens to him before you finish this, it won't die with him." Odette wiped at her eyes with the back of her wrist, composing herself with visible effort. "I should have trusted you both to understand why I was being careful instead of letting you spend three days suspecting the worst of me. That's the part I'm actually sorry for."

Mara squeezed her hand, feeling the last of the suspicion drain out of her, replaced by something that ached in an entirely different way, the specific tenderness of watching an old woman try to protect a dying man's legacy the only way she knew how. "I'm glad it's this," she said quietly. "I'm sorry I doubted you. I don't know how to not chase every shadow right now."

"Don't apologize for that. Not in this valley, not with what you're both up against." Odette managed a small, tired smile, the first genuine one Mara had seen from her all week. "Chase every shadow you need to. Just leave room, when you find one, to be glad it turned out to be me and not him."

Outside, the evening had settled into the particular gold-blue hush that came right before true dark, ordinary and undramatic, and Mara walked out of the store with Caleb's hand in hers, lighter by one suspicion and heavier by the reminder, freshly delivered, of exactly how little time her grandfather had left to finish what he'd started.

"We should go see him," Caleb said quietly, once they were outside, the evening air cool after the closeness of the back office. "Tonight, if his aide will allow it. I don't think either of us wants to sit with this news until morning."

Mara nodded, already reaching for her keys, and found, as they walked to the truck, that some of the day's exhaustion had loosened its grip, replaced by a clearer, steadier resolve than she'd felt in days. Suspicion, at least the wrong kind of it, had a way of costing more than it ever gave back, and she made herself a quiet promise, walking across the darkening lot, to spend whatever was left of this fight aiming it more carefully.
