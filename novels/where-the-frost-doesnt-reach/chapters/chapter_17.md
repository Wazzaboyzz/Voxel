# CHAPTER SEVENTEEN

Dev slept for most of the afternoon, and Mara spent the hours watching Priya sit vigil at his bedside in Caleb's guest room, refusing every offer to be relieved, as though four years of not knowing had earned her the right to simply be present now for as long as she wanted.

He woke a little past six, color better after fluids and a proper meal, though the hollowness around his eyes told Mara it would take considerably longer than one afternoon to undo. He asked for all three of them, and once they'd gathered, chairs pulled close around the bed, he began again, more steady this time, more willing to give the whole shape of it instead of the fragments exhaustion had allowed before.

"The ledger I found," he said, "wasn't just names. It was accounting. Literal accounting, columns and totals, tracking exactly how much time had been redirected, from whom, to whom, going back generations. Kell didn't invent the system. He inherited it, same as he inherited everything else about this valley's arrangement, and near as I can tell, he's spent forty years trying to manage a debt that was already unsustainable before he ever took it over."

"Trying to manage it, or trying to hide that he can't," Caleb said.

"Both, I think. I don't know if he even sees the difference anymore." Dev shifted against his pillows, wincing slightly. "The ledger showed the debt compounding faster than any single redirect could keep pace with. That's why he needed more than one Finder over the years. That's why he needed Elias Frey decades ago, and it's why he needed me, and it's why, God help her, he's already started circling Wren."

Priya's grip on the bed rail tightened. "What do you mean, circling."

"He tests people before he uses them properly. Small asks first, favors that seem harmless, building trust and obligation slowly enough that by the time he asks for something real, refusing feels impossible." Dev's voice had gone hard with old, bitter knowledge. "He started with me the same way. Little errands. A reading here, a favor there, nothing that felt like it cost anything, until suddenly I owed him enough small favors that the big one felt like the only way to settle the account."

Mara felt something cold settle in her stomach, the particular dread of watching a pattern reveal itself a step ahead of where they'd caught up to it. "Has he approached Wren directly yet?"

"I don't know for certain. I've been in that silo for days, and before that I was too careful to let myself get close enough to watch him operate without him noticing me watching." Dev's jaw tightened. "But he mentioned her, once, when he came to check on me. Said something about how nice it was that the valley kept producing young Finders right when he needed one, like the timing was providence instead of exactly the kind of thing a careful man arranges for himself."

"How does he even find them," Mara asked. "The young ones. It's not like a gift like that comes with a public registry."

"He doesn't have to find them exactly. This town does it for him. A kid with a knack for locating lost things gets noticed fast in a valley this small, whispered about at church socials, mentioned carefully at the diner counter. By the time a Finder's gift fully manifests, usually somewhere around puberty, half the town already knows who they are before the family's even had time to explain it to the kid properly." Dev's mouth twisted with old bitterness. "Kell's had decades of informal scouting done for him for free, just by living in a place that can't help but gossip about anything unusual."

"That's monstrous," Priya said quietly.

"It's efficient," Dev corrected, something hollow in his voice. "Which is worse, honestly. Monstrous implies he has to work at it. This just runs on its own, this town feeding him exactly what he needs generation after generation without anyone ever quite realizing what they're doing."

"We need to warn her," Priya said immediately, already halfway to standing.

"You will. Tonight, if you need to." Dev caught his sister's sleeve, gentle but urgent. "But you need the rest of this first, because it changes what warning her actually looks like. He doesn't just take time, not always. Sometimes what he takes is more specific. Memory. Attachment. Whatever's most useful to erase, depending on what he needs the person to stop noticing or stop feeling." His eyes moved to Caleb. "That's what happened to you. He didn't just steal two years at random. He aimed for exactly the years where your bond with Mara would have been strongest, because breaking that bond served a second purpose beyond the raw time itself."

The room went very quiet, and Mara felt the sentence land in her chest like something physical, a confirmation of every fear she'd carried since finding a ring in a drawer that had no business being empty.

"Why," Caleb said, his voice low and dangerous now, the careful composure of the last hour finally starting to crack. "Why specifically us."

"Because your family's bloodline runs old in this valley, same as the Vosses, same as the founding families the original bargain was struck between. Kell's spent decades worried that if the two lines ever properly reconciled, if a Reyes and a Voss actually built the kind of life together that generations before you never got the chance to, it would create something he couldn't predict or control. A concentration of old blood and old loyalty strong enough to actually challenge him, instead of just enough people scattered too thin to organize." Dev's voice had gone rough with the effort of saying it plainly. "He didn't take your years because he needed them randomly. He took them because breaking your engagement served him twice over. Time, and insurance."

Mara felt Caleb's hand find hers, gripping hard enough that she understood, without needing to look at his face, exactly how much this particular truth cost him to hear.

"So this was never only about the debt," Mara said slowly, the shape of the whole design finally, fully visible. "It was also about making sure Caleb and I never became whatever he was afraid we'd become together."

"Yes." Dev held her gaze, something like genuine remorse in his exhausted face. "I'm sorry. I should have found a way to refuse. I keep telling myself there wasn't one, and some days I even believe it, but I think the truth is I was too afraid of what he'd do to Priya if I didn't cooperate, and I let that fear make the decision instead of finding the courage to make a harder one."

"You're not the villain in this story, Dev," Mara said quietly, meaning it more than she expected to, watching the shape of Kell's forty years of careful cruelty rearrange itself into something considerably larger and more calculated than she'd understood even an hour ago. "He is. Every single choice you made was inside a trap he built specifically to make refusing feel impossible. That's not the same as choosing it freely."

"I appreciate you saying that. I don't know yet if I believe it about myself." Dev looked down at his own hands, still faintly marked from the zip ties, turning them over like he was inspecting damage on something other than his own body. "I keep thinking about the version of me that found that ledger four years ago. He thought he was about to do something brave, expose what he'd found, force this town to reckon with it honestly. Instead I spent four years being exactly the kind of quiet, complicit tool Kell needed me to be. I don't know how to reconcile the man who found that ledger with the man who spent four years helping bury what it proved."

"Maybe you don't reconcile them," Caleb said, something unexpectedly gentle in his voice given everything he'd just learned about his own two stolen years. "Maybe you just let both of them be true. The man who found it, and the man who got scared enough to help hide it. People aren't required to be only one version of themselves to be worth saving."

Dev looked at him for a long moment, something in his exhausted face shifting, the particular relief of being told, by the person with every right to hate him most, that redemption wasn't already foreclosed.

Priya finally spoke again, her voice steadier now, the deputy reasserting herself over the sister with visible, deliberate effort. "Then we stop treating this like an investigation into one man's corruption and start treating it like what it actually is. A system he's been running for a century, built to survive exactly this kind of exposure by making sure nobody ever has enough proof, or enough courage, to bring the whole thing down at once."

"So we get both," Caleb said, something hard and resolved settling into his voice. "The proof, and the courage. Whatever it costs."

Dev nodded slowly, exhaustion and relief warring visibly across his face, a man finally allowed to set down a weight he'd carried alone for far too long. "Start with Wren. Whatever else we do, we can't let him finish what he's started with her the way he finished it with me."

"There's one more thing," he added, quieter now, the last of his energy visibly draining out of him. "The ledger. I hid it before he caught me with it, somewhere he's never thought to look because he doesn't know I ever made a copy. If you can get to it, you'll have more than my word against his. You'll have a hundred years of his own bookkeeping."

Priya sat forward sharply. "Where."

"Not tonight." Dev's eyes were already drifting closed again, his body finally surrendering to the exhaustion he'd been holding off through sheer force of will. "Tonight I need to sleep in a bed that isn't a dirt floor, and you all need to figure out how to protect Wren before anything else. The ledger's waited four years. It can wait one more night."

Priya looked like she wanted to argue, every instinct in her straining toward the next piece of evidence, but she caught herself, visibly choosing her brother's exhausted, present safety over the case's momentum. "One more night," she agreed. "Then you tell us everything."

Caleb rose from his chair, motioning for Mara to follow him out and give Dev the quiet he clearly needed, and at the door Mara looked back once at the two siblings, Priya already settling back into the chair beside the bed, unwilling, even now, to leave her brother's side for longer than the walk to the door required.
