# CHAPTER SIXTEEN

The old grain silo sat at the edge of what used to be the Hargrove property before the family sold off the working farmland decades ago, a rusted, listing tower visible from the county road if you knew to look for it through the trees, easy to mistake for one more piece of abandoned agricultural equipment slowly returning itself to the ground. Priya parked the cruiser well short of it, on the theory that an engine's approach carried further out here than any of them wanted to risk.

The walk in was quiet, the three of them moving through knee-high grass gone gold and dry with the season, Toll left behind at the orchard house at Caleb's insistence, unwilling to risk a bark at the wrong moment giving away more than any of them could afford. Mara found herself cataloguing the terrain out of old habit, sightlines, cover, the places a person could hide or be hidden without anyone driving past ever noticing, and understood, doing it, that some part of her had already accepted this was no longer simply a search. It was a rescue, if they were lucky enough to still be in time for one.

"He mentioned this place twice in the notebook," she said, walking the last stretch on foot, her hand resting, Mara noticed, close to her hip in a way that told her exactly how much Priya no longer trusted this valley to be as empty as it looked. "Said it was quiet enough to work through a difficult reading without interruption. If he came here to think through whatever Kell showed him, and if something went wrong while he was doing it."

She didn't finish the sentence, and none of them made her.

The silo's access door hung slightly ajar, rust bleeding down the seam in long orange streaks, and Caleb reached it first, easing it open with a low groan of metal that seemed, in the day's stillness, loud enough to carry for miles. Inside, the light fell in thin gray bars through gaps in the curved roof, dust hanging visible and unmoving in the still air, and it took Mara's eyes a moment to adjust enough to make sense of the shape slumped against the far wall.

"Dev." Priya's voice broke on the name, and she was moving before anyone could stop her, crossing the silo's dirt floor at a near run.

He was alive. Mara registered that first, the specific relief of it landing before anything else could, a man thinner than his photograph, older around the eyes than four missing years alone should account for, wrists bound in front of him with zip ties gone tight enough to have left deep, angry grooves in the skin beneath. His eyes opened slowly at the sound of his sister's voice, unfocused at first, then sharpening with a disbelief that looked almost painful to hold.

"Priya." His voice came out cracked, disused. "You shouldn't be here. If he finds out you came looking."

"I don't care what he finds out." Priya was already working at the zip ties with a knife from her belt, her hands shaking despite the steadiness in her voice, four years of composure finally cracking wide open now that the thing she'd feared and hoped for in equal measure had turned out to be true. "You're alive. You're actually alive. I need you to just be alive for one more minute before you tell me anything else."

Dev let her cut him free, flexing his hands slowly, wincing at the returning circulation, and Mara crouched beside them, checking him with the practiced eye of someone who'd sat through enough job-site first aid trainings to know what to look for. Dehydrated, clearly. Malnourished, though not critically. Bruising along one side of his jaw that looked days old rather than fresh.

"How long," Mara asked gently.

"I don't know exactly. Days, I think. He brings water sometimes. Not food, not really, just enough to keep me from dying before he decides what to do with me." Dev's eyes moved to Caleb, and something in his exhausted face sharpened with recognition. "You're Reyes. Caleb Reyes."

"You know me?"

"I know what happened to you. I'm the reason it happened to you." Dev's voice cracked, shame and exhaustion tangled together so completely Mara couldn't tell where one ended and the other began. "Kell made me do it. Two years ago. He said it was the only way to keep the debt from collapsing on the whole valley at once, said if I didn't help redirect it onto someone, it would take from a hundred people instead of one, and he made it sound like mercy instead of what it actually was."

Caleb had gone very still, and Mara watched him absorb the sentence with a control that clearly cost him everything he had, his jaw working once before he trusted himself to speak. "You're the one who took my years."

"I'm the one who moved them. I didn't create the debt. I didn't choose who paid it." Dev's eyes were wet now, pleading in a way that made Mara's chest ache despite everything. "I've hated myself every single day since. That's not an excuse. I know it's not an excuse. But I need you to know I never once believed it was right, only that he'd convinced me it was necessary, and I was too much of a coward to find another way."

"Why you," Priya asked, still gripping her brother's hand like she could anchor him to the present moment through sheer physical contact. "Out of every Finder in this valley. Why did he choose you."

"Because I found something four years ago I wasn't supposed to find. An old ledger, hidden in the mill's foundation, records going back generations, more names than anyone in this town has ever been told about." Dev's breath came ragged. "He caught me with it. Instead of hurting me outright, he gave me a choice. Help him manage the debt quietly, or watch him make an example of someone I loved to keep the rest of the valley properly frightened into silence." His eyes found Priya's. "I chose you. I've spent four years choosing you, every single day, and I still don't know if that makes me better than him or exactly the same."

"It makes you someone who was put in an impossible position by a man who's had a century of practice making people choose between two forms of the same cruelty," Mara said, firm, refusing to let self-hatred take root in the silence that followed. "That's not the same as being him."

Caleb had not spoken again since learning the truth, and Mara watched him now, watched the visible effort of a man deciding, in real time, what to do with an anger that had every right to consume the whole room. When he finally crouched down to Dev's level, his voice came out steadier than Mara expected, though something in it still trembled at the edges.

"I spent two years thinking I was simply broken," he said. "Thinking whatever was missing in me was some kind of personal failing, something wrong with my own mind that nobody would explain properly. I need you to understand that finding out it was stolen instead of broken doesn't erase the anger. But it does change where I'm pointing it." He held Dev's gaze. "I don't forgive you yet. I don't know if I will. But I understand, and understanding is further than I expected to get today."

Dev's composure finally broke at that, a rough, exhausted sound escaping him that was half sob, half something like relief, and Priya's arm tightened around her brother's shoulders, holding him upright through it.

Dev looked at her, something raw and grateful passing across his face, and then his gaze slid past her, toward the silo's open door, and every trace of relief drained out of him at once.

"You need to go," he said, urgent now, struggling to sit up straighter despite his exhaustion. "Now. Before he comes back to check on me. If he finds you here, all of you, together."

"We're not leaving you here again," Priya said fiercely.

"You're not leaving me here at all. I'm coming with you." Dev pushed himself up, unsteady, refusing Caleb's offered hand at first out of what looked like pure stubborn shame before finally, visibly, letting himself accept the help. "But we need to move fast, and quietly, because if he realizes I've told you any of this before I'm ready to tell you all of it properly, he's not going to give any of us a second chance to walk out of whatever room we're standing in next."

They half carried him between them, out into the gray afternoon light, and Mara found herself glancing back once at the silo's dark opening, feeling the specific, cold certainty that whatever they'd just started here was far from finished, and that the fuller truth Dev had promised was going to cost all of them considerably more than this first fragment already had.

The walk back to the cruiser felt longer than the walk in, Dev's weight heavier against Caleb's shoulder with every step, his bare feet stumbling over ground he clearly hadn't the strength to navigate cleanly on his own. Priya kept up a low, steady stream of talk beside him, nothing important, just the sound of her voice filling the space where silence might otherwise let fear back in, updates on people he hadn't seen in years, a running account of small, ordinary town gossip meant, Mara understood, less to inform him than to remind him what an ordinary conversation was supposed to sound like again.

At the cruiser, Priya laid her jacket across the back seat before easing her brother into it, her hands lingering a moment longer than strictly necessary on his shoulder once he was settled, as though she needed the physical proof of him still being solid and present before she could fully trust it.

"Hospital," she said, sliding into the driver's seat. "I don't care what he says about staying quiet. He needs fluids and a doctor before he needs anything else."

"No hospital." Dev's voice came out weak but firm from the back seat. "Hospitals ask questions, file reports, and the first report that mentions my name is the first thing that tells Kell exactly how much trouble he's actually in. I'll take fluids. I'll take rest. I won't take a paper trail he can follow straight back to whoever helped me."

Priya's jaw tightened, torn between her brother's safety and his logic, and Mara watched her make the calculation the same way she'd watch anyone weigh an unbearable trade-off, slow, reluctant, and ultimately decisive.

"The orchard house, then," Priya said. "Caleb's got the room, and enough distance from town that nobody's likely to come asking questions before we're ready for them to."

Caleb nodded once, already reaching for his phone to warn nobody in particular, simply needing the motion of doing something useful with his hands. "Whatever he needs. We'll figure out the rest once he's actually safe."

Dev's eyes had drifted half shut against the seat, exhaustion finally winning out over adrenaline, but his hand found his sister's over the console, gripping it with what little strength he had left.

"Thank you," he murmured, barely audible over the engine catching to life. "For not giving up on finding me, even after four years."

"Never," Priya said, her voice fierce and thick all at once. "Not for one single day of it."
